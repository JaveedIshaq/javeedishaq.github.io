# Company Research Checklist

## Goal

Research each company before applying or interviewing so your answers feel targeted rather than generic.

Most candidates skip this.
That is why most candidates sound interchangeable.

## What To Learn Before Applying

### 1. Product

Know:

- what the company sells
- who the user is
- what the user is trying to do
- whether the product is B2B, B2C, internal tooling, SaaS, marketplace, or ops-heavy

### 2. Business Model

Know:

- how they make money
- what behavior likely matters to revenue
- what user workflows are probably critical

### 3. Technical Shape

Look for clues about:

- web-heavy product
- mobile-heavy product
- admin-heavy operations
- AI features
- data-heavy workflows
- real-time systems

### 4. Role Requirements

Extract:

- must-have stack keywords
- repeated delivery expectations
- collaboration expectations
- architecture expectations

### 5. Team And Stage

Know:

- startup or established
- small engineering team or larger org
- product maturity level
- likely speed vs process expectations

## What To Learn Before The Interview

### Product Surfaces

Try to identify:

- landing page
- main product
- admin or internal ops patterns
- onboarding experience
- visible product strengths and weaknesses

### AI Clues

Look for:

- search
- recommendation
- assistant/copilot features
- workflow automation
- document handling
- support tooling

### Engineering Clues

Look at:

- careers page
- engineering blog
- product changelog
- public team pages
- GitHub if relevant

## Questions You Should Answer For Yourself

1. Why does this company need someone like me?
2. Which part of my stack is most relevant here?
3. Which project of mine maps best to their product?
4. Where would practical AI actually matter in their system?
5. What kind of product problems are they likely struggling with?

## Fast Research Template

Use this template before every serious interview:

```text
Company:
Product:
Primary users:
Business model:
Likely key workflows:
Likely tech priorities:
Relevant stack match from my background:
Best project to discuss:
Where AI likely matters:
Questions I should ask them:
```

## Good Questions To Ask Them

### Product Questions

- What user workflow matters most to the team right now?
- Where does the product currently feel the most operational pain?
- What is the next major capability the team is trying to ship?

### Engineering Questions

- How do you balance speed of delivery with maintainability?
- Where does the current architecture create the most friction?
- How do product and engineering collaborate on scope decisions?

### AI Questions

- Are there places where AI is already part of the workflow?
- If not, where do you think AI could create real value without adding noise?

## Red Flags To Watch For

- vague role with unclear ownership
- “full-stack” role that is mostly maintenance churn
- AI mentioned only as hype
- no clear product direction
- role asks for everything with no focus

## Final Rule

Company research is not about sounding flattering.

It is about proving:

- you understand their problem space
- you know where your value fits
- you are not applying blindly
