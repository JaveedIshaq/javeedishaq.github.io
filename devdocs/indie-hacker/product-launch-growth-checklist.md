# Product Launch Growth Checklist
### What a Mobile App or Digital Product Needs Beyond Development

> **Brutal truth:** building the app is only half the work. A product that nobody discovers, understands, or trusts will not sell just because it is technically good.

This document is the minimum go-to-market checklist for any app or product you ship.

---

## The Five Growth Layers

Every product needs these five layers:

1. **Clear positioning**
2. **Discoverability**
3. **Conversion**
4. **Distribution**
5. **Retention**

If one layer is weak, growth becomes much harder.

---

## 1. Clear Positioning

Before SEO, social, or ads, answer this:

- Who is the product for?
- What painful problem does it solve?
- Why is it better than doing nothing?
- Why is it better than the current workaround?
- Why should someone trust this product now?

### Minimum positioning assets

- one-line product promise
- short pitch for website and App Store
- 3 to 5 use cases
- ideal customer profile
- clear screenshots showing value, not just UI

### Example

Weak:

- "AI app for creators"

Better:

- "Create short-form video reels from a script, voice note, or product link in minutes."

## 2. Search Engine Optimization

SEO matters for web apps, landing pages, waitlists, blogs, and product discovery through Google.

### What you need

- a dedicated landing page for each product
- a strong page title
- a clear meta description
- one primary keyword focus per page
- proper headings
- fast page speed
- mobile-friendly layout
- internal links
- FAQ content
- schema markup when useful

### Practical SEO checklist

- choose 3 primary keywords and 10 secondary keywords
- include the main keyword in the title, URL, H1, and first paragraph
- write pages for real search intent, not keyword stuffing
- create comparison pages when relevant
- publish support articles, use cases, and tutorials
- add OG tags for sharing
- make screenshots, demo GIFs, and visuals load fast

### Good page types to create

- homepage
- features page
- pricing page
- use-case pages
- comparison pages
- blog/tutorial pages
- changelog page

### What to avoid

- generic AI buzzwords with no buyer intent
- thin pages with no useful substance
- duplicate copy across pages
- waiting until launch to think about SEO

## 3. AI Search Optimization and Indexing

This is different from classic SEO.

The goal is to make your product easy to understand, quote, and recommend by AI systems, search assistants, and answer engines.

### What helps AI discover and understand your product

- clear, factual landing page copy
- structured feature lists
- public pricing
- public FAQ
- product comparisons
- documentation pages
- predictable headings
- strong entity clarity around what the product does

### AI discoverability checklist

- write a simple product description in plain language
- make pricing and target users visible
- create FAQ pages answering real buying questions
- add comparison content like "`X vs Y`"
- publish use cases with concrete examples
- keep product docs crawlable
- avoid vague marketing language with no substance
- make sure your site has consistent naming across pages

### Indexing basics

- submit sitemap to Google Search Console
- ensure important pages are indexable
- avoid blocking useful docs or landing pages
- use canonical URLs where needed
- add structured data for software/app where appropriate

### The practical rule

If a human cannot understand your product in 20 seconds, AI systems will not explain it well either.

## 4. App Store Optimization

If you ship a mobile app, ASO is not optional.

### Core ASO assets

- app name
- subtitle or short description
- keyword strategy
- icon
- screenshots
- preview video
- strong first 3 screenshots
- ratings and reviews plan

### ASO checklist

- choose a strong app title with a real keyword
- make the first screenshot explain the main value
- write benefits, not feature dumps
- localize later if traction justifies it
- test icon and screenshot variations
- ask early users for reviews at the right moment
- keep release notes clear and professional

### What matters most

- click-through rate from listing
- install conversion rate
- ratings quality
- retention after install

### Mistakes

- ugly screenshots
- generic titles
- no preview video
- asking for reviews too early
- shipping without analytics

## 5. Social Media Presence and Social Marketing

Social is not just for vanity. It is for trust, discovery, and repetition.

### Pick only a few channels

For your style and products, the most practical channels are:

- `LinkedIn` for credibility and professional distribution
- `X/Twitter` for builder updates and networking
- `Instagram` or `TikTok` for reels and visual distribution
- `YouTube Shorts` for demos and educational clips

Do not try to dominate every platform at once.

### What to post

- build-in-public updates
- before/after clips
- feature demos
- customer problems and solutions
- short lessons from building
- launch countdowns
- clips showing actual product value

### Content system

Turn one product update into multiple formats:

- one reel
- one carousel or image post
- one short text post
- one founder-style lesson

### Social marketing checklist

- secure consistent usernames
- create a product logo and simple brand kit
- make profile bios say what the product does
- pin one strong intro post
- post product proof, not only motivation
- collect emails, not only likes
- always link back to the landing page

### What to avoid

- content with no product link
- random motivational posting
- posting only features with no user story
- building pages and then going silent

## 6. Launch Assets You Actually Need

Before launch, prepare these:

- landing page
- demo video
- screenshots
- one-line pitch
- longer product description
- pricing structure
- FAQ
- privacy policy
- terms page
- support email or support page
- waitlist or email capture
- analytics setup

---

## 7. Retention and Trust

People do not just buy products. They buy confidence.

### Trust builders

- clean onboarding
- good docs
- fast support replies
- transparent pricing
- changelog
- roadmap or feature request process
- social proof if available

### Retention basics

- measure activation
- measure 7-day retention
- ask why users stop
- improve onboarding before adding many features

---

## 8. The Real Indie-Hacker Rule

Do not launch just code.

Launch:

- a product
- a message
- a distribution path
- a trust story

Without these, even a strong app stays invisible.

---

## Your Minimum Launch Stack

For each product you build, aim to have:

- product idea file
- landing page
- demo video
- keyword list
- social handles
- app store copy if mobile
- FAQ and docs
- 10 launch posts or content angles

If these do not exist, the product is not ready for real market testing.
